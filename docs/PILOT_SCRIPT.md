# WAQT — Pilot & Discovery Script

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Turn a warm supermarket into your first paying customer (and first proof) |
| **Product** | WAQT — expiry-date inventory + Supplier Return |
| **Audience** | Founder running the meeting |
| **Date** | 2026-08-22 |

> Goal of the whole process: get the supermarket's **real numbers**, run a short **pilot** that proves WAQT recovers money, then **convert to RM 2,000/month**. You are selling *money recovered*, not software.

---

## Stage 0 — Before the meeting (prep)

- Pick **one warm supermarket** and identify two people: the **owner/director** (signs the cheque) and the **operations/store manager** (feels the pain daily).
- Bring: a laptop with a simple WAQT demo (or the reference screens), a notebook, and this script.
- Mindset: this first meeting is **90% listening, 10% pitching**. You are diagnosing, not selling yet.
- Book **45 minutes**.

---

## Stage 1 — Discovery meeting (≈45 min)

### 1a. Open (2 min)
> "Thank you for the time. I'm building **WAQT**, a simple tool that helps supermarkets stop losing money on expired stock — especially getting that money back from suppliers. Before I show anything, I'd love to understand how you handle expiry today. Is that okay?"

### 1b. Understand their current process (10 min) — just listen
- "Walk me through what happens today when a product on the shelf is close to its expiry date. Who notices it, and how?"
- "Does your current system (the one on your computer) track expiry dates at all?" *(You already saw it doesn't — let them confirm it.)*
- "When something expires, what do you do with it — throw it away, or send it back to the supplier?"
- "Who handles that return to the supplier? How long does it take?"

### 1c. Find the pain & the money (12 min) — the important part
Ask gently, write down every number:
- "Roughly how much do you spend on stock each month?" → **RM ______ / month**
- "Of that, how much is products that can expire (food, personal care, health & beauty)?" → **RM ______ / month**
- "In a normal month, how much stock expires before you sell it?" → **RM ______ / month** *(if they don't know — that's a key finding: "you can't see it today")*
- "When stock expires, roughly what portion do you successfully return to the supplier for credit — and what portion you just throw away?" → **____% returned / ____% thrown away**
- "Has an inspector/authority ever found expired stock on your shelf? What happened?" *(compliance pain)*
- "How much time does your staff spend checking dates and doing returns each week?" → **____ hours/week**

### 1d. Reflect it back (3 min)
> "So if I understand right: you're spending about **RM ___ a month** on stock that can expire, roughly **RM ___** of it expires, and you're only getting **__%** of that back from suppliers — the rest is money lost. And nobody can see it clearly until it's too late. Did I get that right?"

*(Let them agree. This shared number becomes the whole business case.)*

### 1e. Show WAQT (10 min)
Keep it to the loop that matters:
1. **See everything expiring** — the expiry board (Expired / soon / later), per store.
2. **Get warned early** — the alert (email/in-app) before it expires.
3. **Get the money back** — create a **Supplier Return**, send to the supplier, track the credit recovered.
4. **Prove it** — the report showing **RM recovered** and waste avoided.

> "The whole point of WAQT is this: see it early, act on it, and claim the money back from your supplier — and show you exactly how much you recovered."

### 1f. Propose the pilot (5 min)
> "I'd like to prove this with your real stock, not just slides. Let's run a short pilot."

Go to Stage 2.

---

## Stage 2 — The pilot offer

**Structure (say this plainly):**
- **Duration:** 8 weeks.
- **Scope:** start with **1–2 of your stores** and your expiring categories.
- **Cost:** **free (or heavily discounted) during the pilot** — you only pay if it works.
- **What I need from you:** your product + supplier list (export from your system), and someone on your team to use it for receiving + returns.
- **The deal / success metric:**
  > "If, in 8 weeks, WAQT helps you **recover at least RM ___ from suppliers** (and shows you the expiry you were blind to before), you continue at **RM 2,000/month**. If it doesn't, you owe nothing and we part as friends."
- **In return I ask for:** an honest testimonial and permission to use you as a reference for other supermarkets.

**Set the baseline (critical):**
> "So we can prove the difference, let's first write down where you are today: how much expired last quarter, and how much you got back from suppliers. That's our starting line."

Record: **Baseline expired RM/quarter = ______**, **Baseline % recovered = ______**.

---

## Stage 3 — Pilot kickoff (setup day)

- Import their product + supplier list into WAQT (their master data).
- Create their **stores**; invite their staff (admin + staff roles).
- Train them on 3 actions only: **receive stock with expiry date**, **read the expiry board/alerts**, **create a Supplier Return**.
- Agree who checks WAQT daily and who processes returns weekly.
- Confirm the alert email goes to the right person.

**Keep it small.** Don't teach every feature — just the loop that makes money.

---

## Stage 4 — During the pilot (8 weeks)

Track weekly (this becomes your results deck):
- RM of expiring stock **flagged in time**.
- Number of **Supplier Returns created** and **RM credit recovered**.
- RM **scrapped** (couldn't be returned).
- Any "expired on shelf" incidents avoided.

Check in with the manager every 1–2 weeks (WhatsApp is fine). Fix friction fast — the #1 risk is they stop entering data. Make receiving quick.

---

## Stage 5 — Results review & close (≈30 min)

Bring one simple before/after:

| | Before WAQT (baseline) | During pilot (8 weeks) |
|---|---|---|
| Expired stock value | RM ______ | RM ______ |
| Recovered from suppliers | RM ______ (__%) | **RM ______ (__%)** |
| Thrown away (lost) | RM ______ | RM ______ |
| Expired-on-shelf incidents | ______ | ______ |

> "Over 8 weeks WAQT helped you recover **RM ___** you would have lost, and gave you visibility you didn't have. At **RM 2,000/month**, that pays for itself in about **___ weeks**. Shall we continue, and roll it out to your other stores?"

**Then:** confirm start date, billing, and which stores to expand to (land-and-expand). Ask for the testimonial and a referral to another supermarket owner.

---

## Objection handling (quick answers)

- **"It's too expensive."** → "Let's look at your own numbers: you're losing RM ___ a month. WAQT costs RM 2,000 and recovered RM ___ in the pilot. It pays for itself." *(Always return to their numbers.)*
- **"My staff won't use it."** → "That's exactly why the pilot exists — we keep it to 3 simple actions and I train your team. If they won't use it, you don't pay."
- **"My current system already does inventory."** → "It does — but it doesn't track expiry or handle supplier returns. WAQT sits alongside it, it doesn't replace it. We even import your product list from it."
- **"Let me think about it."** → "Of course. The pilot is free and low-risk — the only cost of waiting is another few weeks of expired stock. Can we at least set the baseline this week?"
- **"Can you do it cheaper?"** → Hold the price; offer scope (start with fewer stores) or annual billing with a discount, not a lower monthly rate that anchors you low.
